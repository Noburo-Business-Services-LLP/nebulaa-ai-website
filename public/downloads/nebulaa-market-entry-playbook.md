# New Market Entry Playbook

**How to build demand in a city before your product is on the shelf.**

Nebulaa · nebulaa.ai · hello@nebulaa.ai

---

## The mistake this is written to prevent

Most brands entering a new city sort distribution first and marketing second.
Stock arrives, sits, and the post-mortem concludes the market "wasn't ready."

The market was fine. Nobody there had heard of you.

Demand generation has a lead time. Awareness built in the four weeks before
launch is worth a fraction of awareness built in the four months before it,
because familiarity compounds and a cold audience does not convert on week one.

This playbook is the sequence we run on live market-entry engagements,
generalised so you can run it yourself.

---

## Before month one — decide three things

**One city.** Not a state, not a region. A city you can name retail clusters
inside. Multi-city entry is the same playbook run in parallel, and it costs
proportionally more — it is not cheaper per market.

**One launch date.** Everything sequences backwards from it. If the date is
genuinely unknown, you are not ready to start this; pick a target and accept
that it will move.

**What "available" will mean.** Quick commerce only? General trade? Modern
trade? A brand discoverable on Blinkit but absent from neighbourhood stores
needs different content from one that is on shelf everywhere.

---

## Month 1 — Build the market

The goal is existence, not conversion. Nobody buys this month.

- Start city-focused organic content. Local references, local language where it
  matters, and the founding story — which travels further in a new market than
  it does in a home one.
- Run geo-targeted awareness campaigns tightly bounded to the city, and ideally
  to the neighbourhoods you expect to stock first.
- Identify regional creators. Not national names — creators whose audience is
  physically in this city. Follower count matters far less than location.
- Map quick-commerce availability. Which platforms serve which pin codes, and
  what your listing will need.
- Agree priority retail clusters with whoever handles your distribution.

**What good looks like:** people in the city have encountered the brand,
even if they could not yet buy it.

---

## Month 2 — Build product interest

Move from "this brand exists" to "I would try that."

- Shift content from brand to product. Use cases, ingredients, how it is made,
  what it goes with.
- Brief and run the first creator collaborations. Their audience meeting your
  product is worth more than any amount of your own posting.
- Begin quick-commerce discovery activity where you are listed.
- Start sampling in priority neighbourhoods — small volume, high frequency,
  in the places you will actually be stocked.

**What good looks like:** the first unprompted "where can I buy this"
messages arrive. Answer every one, and record where they came from.

---

## Month 3 — Create purchase intent

Demand and availability have to converge. This is the month they do.

- Intensify performance campaigns. Shift budget toward the areas producing
  enquiries rather than spreading evenly.
- Push offer and trial communication — a reason to try now rather than later.
- Drive traffic to live listings. A listing with no traffic pointed at it is
  not distribution, it is inventory.
- Step up sampling and retail activation as stock lands.
- Complete distribution readiness. Everything after this point assumes product
  is reachable.

**What good looks like:** enquiries are about where and how much, not what.

---

## Month 4 — Launch and convert

- Sustain visibility rather than spiking it. A launch week spike that nothing
  follows is money spent on a moment.
- Keep creators active. Second and third posts from the same creator outperform
  first posts, because their audience has now seen you twice.
- Promote listings where you are available, hard.
- Run retail activation on the ground through launch week.
- Watch response times. A launch generates enquiry volume, and slow replies at
  exactly this moment are the most expensive slow replies you will ever send.

**What good looks like:** you enter with awareness, availability and activity
already running — rather than stock and hope.

---

## What we would not promise you

**That distribution will follow.** Demand generation makes the conversation
with a distributor easier. It does not make it certain, and anyone telling you
otherwise is selling something.

**That four months is a rule.** It is the shape that works. A category with a
short consideration cycle can compress it; a considered purchase may need
longer.

**That this is cheap.** It is cheaper than launching into a market that has
never heard of you and writing off the stock. That is the actual comparison.
