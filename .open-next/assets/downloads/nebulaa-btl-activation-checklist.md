# BTL Activation Checklist

**What actually has to happen before, during and after a sampling day.**

Nebulaa · nebulaa.ai · hello@nebulaa.ai

---

This is written from running activations, which is why it includes the things
that go wrong rather than only the things that should happen. Most activation
failures are not strategy failures. They are a permission nobody asked for, a
promoter who did not show, or stock that arrived on the wrong day.

---

## 1 — Two weeks out: permissions and location

- [ ] **Store or mall permission secured in writing.** Verbal agreement with a
      floor manager is not permission. Get the name of whoever actually
      authorises it.
- [ ] **Society or association permission** if the location is residential.
      This is the one most often skipped and most often enforced.
- [ ] **Local body / municipal permission** where required for outdoor or
      public-space activity. Varies by city — check rather than assume.
- [ ] **Dates confirmed against the store's own calendar.** Avoid their stock
      days and their busiest hours unless footfall is the point.
- [ ] **Space confirmed physically, not on a plan.** Where exactly? How much
      frontage? Is there a power point? Who else is activating that week?

## 2 — Ten days out: stock and materials

- [ ] **Sample stock ordered with a buffer.** Ten to fifteen per cent over
      forecast. Running out at 2pm on a good day is a worse outcome than
      carrying stock back.
- [ ] **Batch dates checked.** Nothing near expiry in a sampling batch.
- [ ] **Standee, table, branding and uniforms confirmed** — produced, not
      quoted.
- [ ] **Leaflets or offer cards** with a working QR or number on them. Test
      the QR with a real phone, not a preview.
- [ ] **Storage sorted.** Where does stock sit overnight between days?
- [ ] **Transport arranged** for stock and materials, both directions.

## 3 — One week out: people

- [ ] **Promoters booked and confirmed,** with one reserve per two promoters.
      Attendance is the single most common point of failure.
- [ ] **Briefing done and written down.** What the product is, the three
      things to say, the two questions they will be asked, what not to claim.
- [ ] **Someone named as on-ground lead** who is not also promoting.
- [ ] **Escalation number circulated** — who they call when something breaks.
- [ ] **Grooming and conduct standards stated.** Obvious until it is not.

## 4 — The day before

- [ ] **Confirm with the store, again.** Staff change, and the person who
      agreed may not be working.
- [ ] **Weather checked** for anything outdoor, with a wet-weather plan that
      is a decision and not an improvisation.
- [ ] **Stock physically at or near the location.**
- [ ] **Promoters re-confirmed by phone,** not message.
- [ ] **Counting method agreed.** How will samples distributed actually be
      recorded? Decide this now, not at 11am tomorrow.

## 5 — On the day

- [ ] **Setup an hour before footfall,** not before opening.
- [ ] **Photograph the setup** before anyone touches it. You will want this.
- [ ] **Record samples distributed hourly.** Hourly, not a total at close —
      hourly data tells you when to staff next time.
- [ ] **Record questions asked.** The recurring question is product feedback
      and belongs in your marketing.
- [ ] **Capture contacts where the category justifies it,** with consent and a
      genuine reason.
- [ ] **Check in with store staff during the day.** They decide whether you are
      invited back.

## 6 — Within 48 hours

- [ ] **Reconcile stock.** Distributed, returned, damaged. Gaps here are how
      you find process problems.
- [ ] **Collate counts, photographs and questions** into one record per
      activation day.
- [ ] **Compare against the digital campaign** running around that catchment in
      the same week. This pairing is the closest thing to attribution BTL has.
- [ ] **Log what went wrong,** honestly. The next activation is only better if
      this list is real.
- [ ] **Thank the store.** Cheap, and it decides the next permission.

---

## On measuring this honestly

BTL will never measure as cleanly as a click, and anyone presenting it as
though it does is rounding. What you can measure reliably:

- Samples distributed, by hour and location
- Contacts captured, where relevant
- Recurring questions and objections
- Store and dealer engagement — did they want you back
- Movement in geo-targeted digital campaigns around the same catchment in the
  same week

That last one is the strongest signal available, and it only exists if the
digital and on-ground activity are planned together. Run separately by
different vendors, they cannot be compared at all.
